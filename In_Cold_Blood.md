# In Cold Blood by Truman Capote

Truman Capote’s In Cold Blood initially follows the perspectives of the Clutter family and their acquaintances, such as Bobby Rupp, before the night of the Clutters’ death. The Clutters go through a relatively normal day, and Bobby, who leaves the house at around 11 PM and is the last to see the Clutters alive, accounts for the night as ‘just as any other night’. Perry and Dick, two criminals who have previously been jailed for crimes such as writing false checks, are also introduced, but it is unclear what their purpose is. Shifting the perspective to Perry and Dick after the night of the murder, however, it becomes obvious to the audience that they are the ones who committed the crime. 

Perry and Dick, after the murder, plan to run away to Mexico to escape the Kansas police and find a job. Meanwhile, the sheriff and Kansas police inspect the site of the murder on the following day to find no leading clues about the identity of the murderers or the motive of the crime. It is only when Floyd Wells, a former cellmate of Dick, confesses that Dick and Perry are the murderers that the police get a leading clue to capturing the murderers.

After countless hours of driving between areas such as Mexico and Miami, the murderers are finally captured in Las Vegas and sent to the Kansas jail for a court hearing. A confession from Perry reveals that the motive for the crime was to rob the Clutters’ alleged safe and that the murder wasn’t intended. After failing to escape death row on account of insanity, they are sentenced to The Corner, where they are eventually hanged. The final words from Dick reveal his acceptance of fate while Perry apologizes for his crimes. 

The book’s narration style was unique and provided a more compelling reading experience than a single-perspective narration. Having multiple perspectives on people’s experience with the murder allowed me to understand the Holcomb case more deeply than if it had been simply narrated from one perspective. After developing a care for the Clutter family by sharing their perspective for the majority of the first part of the book, their death made me hooked and determined to find out the murderer’s intentions. Including Perry and Dick’s backstory about their tragic and harsh backstory also allowed me to sympathize with the murderer’s situation. In addition, I enjoyed how the book initially didn’t reveal the murder procedure. This choice by the author left me compelled to continue reading to answer the many questions I had about the murder, such as their motivation for the murder and why they chose the Clutters specifically. Through the book’s narration style, I had a much more enjoyable and emotional reading experience.
