# Pride and Prejudice by Jane Austen

Pride and Prejudice by Jane Austen follows the perspective of Elizabeth Bennet and recounts her family’s path in marrying off the daughters. In the setting of Pride and Prejudice, marriage with a wealthy man symbolizes respect and admiration. So when Mr. Bingley and Mr. Darcy, two men of high income, move into Netherfield, Mrs. Bennet, the mother of Elizabeth Bennet, becomes frantic and hopes to marry off her daughters to these rich men. Jane, the eldest daughter of the Bennets, becomes well acquainted with Mr. Bingley during a ball, although Mr. Darcy is seen as a prideful man unwilling to dance with lower-class individuals. 

Although the marriage between Mr. Bingley and Jane seems probable, attempts by Caroline Bingley, Mr. Bingley’s sister, ultimately force Mr. Bingley to leave Netherfield for London, severing their connection. Things take a turn for the worse when Mr. Collins, the man who will inherit the Bennet estate, marries another woman after being rejected by Elizabeth Bennet. Mrs. Bennet is furious because this marriage breaks the Bennet family’s security, as they now have no chance to keep the estate after Mr. Bennet’s death. 

Fortunately, Elizabeth begins to become acquainted with Mr. Wickham, an officer currently visiting Longbourn. They share many common beliefs, most notably about Mr. Darcy’s arrogance and pride, and Elizabeth begins to respect Mr. Wickham as an admirable person. Eventually, Mr. Wickham leaves due to his regiment, but they plan to stay acquainted and close in the future. 

Elizabeth visits many locations throughout the book. For example, she visits Mr. Collins and his wife at their new house and becomes acquainted with Lady Catherine de Bourgh, a rich lady who is revealed to be the aunt of Mr. Darcy. Unexpectedly, Mr. Darcy proposes to Elizabeth during her stay at the Hunsford Parsonage. Elizabeth, who sees Darcy as a partial motivator for Mr. Bingley’s leave to London and an arrogant man, rejects Mr. Darcy, claiming that he is the last person she would ever marry. The following day, Mr. Darcy gives Elizabeth a letter defending himself from her accusations. Most notably, Darcy claims that Mr. Wickham’s accusations against Darcy are false and defends his claim with a past incident regarding Mr. Wickham and Miss Darcy. In addition, Darcy admits to convincing Mr. Bingley to move to London, but only because he saw Mrs. Bennet and her family as wanting him just for his wealth. He claims that he now sees it as a misjudgement on his behalf. 

Later, she visits her uncle and aunt to tour the nearby area. This time, Elizabeth sees Darcy as a more respectful figure, and the two enjoy each other’s company at Darcy’s estate, Pemberley. Urgent business regarding Mr. Wickham and Lydia’s engagement in London brings Elizabeth back home hastily, however. Now, realizing that Mr. Wickham is a devious man, Mr. Bennet and Elizabeth’s uncle search for Lydia in London in hopes of rescuing her in time. Realizing that marriage between the two is the only way to preserve the family’s reputation, Darcy pays Wickham’s requested sum and marries the two off. 

Shortly after Lydia and Mr. Wickham’s marriage, Mr. Darcy and Mr. Bingley come back to Longbourn for a shooting party. This time, Mr. Bingley and Jane get married with Mr. Darcy’s approval. In addition, Elizabeth readily accepts Mr. Darcy’s proposal after seeing him now as a respectful figure. Although initially having no married daughters, Pride and Prejudice ends with three of the five Bennet daughters being married off into society. 

The book was enjoyable, particularly because it is narrated by Elizabeth. This is significant because it allows us to understand Elizabeth’s analysis, which she does often because of her character and personality, and sympathize with her. The built sympathy with Elizabeth allows us to feel the same emotions as she does through shocking events. Most notably, reading about Mr. Darcy’s accusations of Mr. Wickham shocked me as much as it did Elizabeth, just because I had been so acquainted with Elizabeth’s good judgement towards Wickham. In addition, Pride and Prejudice’s massive plot twists, such as Mr. Wickham’s past incident and Mr. Darcy’s respectful nature, kept me on edge. I wanted to continue reading to discover more plot twists and see how the opinions of characters such as Mr. Darcy developed as more people found out about his true nature.
